# Light engine harness

Node scripts that render `src/light` scenes in headless system Chrome (Playwright, `channel: 'chrome'`) through a Vite dev server rooted at the repo (`@` points to `src`). Pixels come back to Node over HTTP; Node writes PNGs and composes sheets with sharp. Every output goes under `artifacts/` (gitignored).

No GPU flags are passed. On macOS headless Chrome uses the Metal GPU and has `EXT_color_buffer_float`. If a machine falls back to software rendering, set `LIGHT_CHROME_ARGS="--enable-gpu --ignore-gpu-blocklist"`.

The harness page loads each recipe file on its own. A recipe that does not load (often because its owner is mid-edit) only fails the scenes that use it: `check.mjs` prints a note and skips it, `audit.mjs` reports those references as failed and goes on, and the export check is skipped because exports load every recipe. If the page itself cannot start, the harness stops at once with the browser's or Vite's message.

References come from `public/references/awe/manifest.json` when it exists (only entries whose file exists), otherwise from the screenshots in `artifacts/brief-screens/` with their white rounded corners masked. Width and height always come from the image file. Presets are read from `src/light/presets/references/<id>.json`.

## Commands

The same scripts run as `npm run light:check`, `npm run light:audit -- --all`, `npm run light:fit -- --ref <id>` and `npm run light:profile -- --recipe <id>` (arguments after `--`).

```sh
node scripts/light/check.mjs [--only hex,determinism,resolution,banding,tone,scatter,context,export,lens] [--out dir]
```

Engine checks in the browser; prints a pass/fail table and exits 1 on a failure. Writes to `artifacts/light-check/`:

- `hex-swatches.png`: flat fields of the 12 pack colors (each must be its exact hex with dither, grain and lens off; also with bloom 0.5; with dither on the mean must be within 0.5 code values).
- `resolution-*.png`: the same scene at 925 px and at the 3840 px long edge, downsampled (mean Oklab ΔE under 0.004 required).
- `banding-ramp-4k.png`, `banding-crop.png`: a 3840x2160 ramp #0B0F14 → #1C2B33 and a 4x crop with and without dither, contrast x8. Reports the banding score, the longest run of equal 8-bit values and how far column means stray from the exact ramp.
- `tone-curve.png`, `tone-curve-onset-zoom.png`: ramps 0 → 16x nominal for every pack color and six primaries, plus glowing dots and lines at 0.5x .. 32x. Checks that the GPU matches the CPU curve, luminance keeps its slope where compression starts, warm colors whiten through yellow (never back toward pink), blues stay within 8° of their hue on the violet side, and hue never jumps.
- `tone-glows.png`: gaussian dots, then vertical gaussian lines, at 1.5x, 3x and 8x nominal for each of the 12 pack colors (one row each, pack order), shown at 2x zoom. This is where a glow crossing 1 would show an edge.
- `tone-ties.png`: Yellow and Orange-red glows at 1.5x, 3x and 8x, alone and overlapping a Cyan and a Meta Blue glow, so the overlaps run through ties of two channels for the largest. The tie check sweeps the second channel through the largest (0.85 to 1 / 0.85 of it in 0.25 % steps) for every pair of channels at 8 intensities and 5 third-channel levels, and fails if any channel moves more than 3 code values in one step. The slope check above skips colors within 5 % of a tie (Ice and the three secondaries), where the curve gives up an exact slope so that it has no ridge.
- `grain-strength-0.5.png`, `grain-strength-1.png`: a black-to-white ramp at grain Density 0.5 and 1, crops of the black end, the middle and the white end at 2x. Also checks that Density 0.5 gives 1 to 2 % Oklab L of grain at L 0.45 in a 4K export, that a 960x540 preview shows the same grain as the 4K export shrunk to 960x540, that a flat grey keeps its mean within 0.1 code values at Density 1, that more pixels change at each higher Density, that the speck size (the correlation of neighbouring pixels) is the same at Density 0.25 and 1, that black stays exactly 0 at Density 1, and that a recipe grain weight of 0.25 gives a quarter of the grain, also through a post pass.
- `scatter-cache.png`: a slow smooth layer at every pixel next to the same layer from an unblurred 128 px scatter source; the check also compares a 925 px render with a 3840 px one and times both at 1920x1080.
- `lens-scatter-16x9.png`, `lens-scatter-9x16.png` (`--only lensscatter`): a scene of sharp bands (wavy outlines, a Yellow band exactly 3 R tall, a thin Ice line) with no blur, the gaussian at sigma 0.53 R, the engine's lens and the CPU prototype's lens, at R 0.0825. The checks compare engine and prototype at R 0.045, 0.0825 and 0.12 at both aspects (the CPU input is the sharp scene rendered 4x larger and averaged 4 x 4, padded by repeating the frame edges), measure a horizontal edge's 10-90 width against the prototype kernel's, read the 3 R band's middle, compare 925 px with 3840 px, and time 1080p and the 4K export render with a cheap scene, a slow scene read only through the lens, and the slow scene also read at every pixel.
- `scatter.png`: the `test.scatter` program (two rims; each disc catches only the other rim's blurred light) with scatter off, at blur 0.06 and at 0.15. Also checks on flat fields that `combine` gets the radiance blur and the aux blur in order, and that the scattered light lands only where `combine` puts it. `test.scatter` is also in the 925 vs 3840 px check.
- `export-vs-preview.png`: a 16:9 export through `exportPreset` (offscreen renderer, JPEG encode; checks size, format and file name) downsampled next to a 960x540 preview of the same preset.
- speed (no image): for sunset.ramp, sunset.blooms and contour.beam, the 1920x1080 1x time at full resolution and at `radianceScale` 0.5; the 0.5 render must look the same as full resolution (p95 under 0.004) and a 960x540 preview must match the 4K export shrunk to 960x540.
- aspect (no image): a probe program records `ctx.aspect` in `uniforms`, `radianceScale`, a post pass and a scatter radius, at export sizes, at preview sizes the tool would make for each format, and at a 925x1002 reference frame. All four must get the format's exact aspect (the reference keeps 925 / 1002).
- context (no image): loses a context on purpose; `readPixels`, `render` and a new `LightRenderer` on that canvas must throw an error starting "WebGL context lost" (so `exportPreset` throws instead of returning a black image). Also prints a 1920x1080 1x render's time with and without `measure: true` (which waits for the GPU with a 1-pixel read).
- `lens.png`: point lights, a line and a disk with the lens off, bloom, halation, and both. Also checks that bloom keeps the mean light of a dim scene.
- `lens-halation.png`, `lens-halation-2.png`: a Meta Blue disk, a thin white-hot line and an orange-red dot at 4x with halation off and on, the blue disk alone at 8x, everything at nominal, at nominal with exposure +2, and at 4x on a bright background. Checks that blue lights at 4x and 8x add no light at all, that white-hot and orange lights get a red-orange halo, that nominal light adds nothing, that exposure counts before the threshold, and that bright surroundings hold the halo back.

Lens rules, all in exposed light (exposure is applied first):
- Bloom has no threshold; it mixes the scene with its blur (`mix(scene, bloom, amount)`, per-level weights summing to 1 and tints of luminance 1), so flat fields stay exact and a dim scene keeps its mean light.
- Halation is fed by each channel's own light above nominal, like film, where only light that reaches the red layer comes back: per sample of the lens base, channel value times a ramp from `halationThreshold` (at least 1) to twice that. `halationTint` is the per-channel strength; the default `#FF9500` is red 1, green 0.3, blue 0, so blue lights make no halo and white-hot lights get orange near, red farther out. Red sits at `halationRadius`, green `halationSpread` x 1.5 levels narrower (default 0.67: one level).
- Halation is added in scene-linear light after bloom, times `1 / (1 + max(Y - 1, 0))` (it does not tint the hot core itself) and `1 / (1 + 4 max(Ys - 0.15, 0))`, where `Ys` is the luminance of the wide surroundings (the scene clamped to nominal, blurred to about 0.06 of the short side). Dark scenes keep the full halo, including inside a recipe's own glow; on a bright background it is held back so it does not tint the background.
- Vignette (`lens.vignette`, 0 by default, up to 1): after bloom and halation and before the tone curve, the exposed light is multiplied by (1 + d²)^(-2 x vignette), where d is the distance from the center scaled to 1 at the corners (a lens's cos⁴ falloff). 1 takes the corners two stops down and the middles of the long edges about 0.6 stop; 0.5 is one stop in the corners. The Lab lists it with the lens settings. `lens-vignette-0.png` (a flat grey) and `lens-vignette-1.png` (sunset.ramp) show 0, 0.5 and 1; the check measures the grey at the center, corners and edge middles against the formula, looks for a step along the diagonal, and compares 925 px with 3840 px.
- `report.json`: every result and the run time.

```sh
node scripts/light/tone-study.mjs [--curves old,engine,camera,...] [--out dir]
```

CPU only, about 2 s. Puts the previous engine tone curve (`old`: straight Oklab line to white at constant hue) next to each alternative that was tried, and prints each pack color's hex and Oklab L, C, h at 1x .. 32x. Writes `artifacts/tone-study/glows-<pack>-<curve>.png` (dots and lines at 1.5x, 3x, 8x, 2x zoom) and `ramps-<curve>.png`. `engine` repeats the constants in `src/light/engine/output.ts`.

The engine curve: below max channel 1 nothing changes. Above it the largest channel stays at 1 and the other channels fill toward 1 like a camera's: each at a rate set by its own light plus crosstalk from the others, with one overall rate that keeps the luminance slope continuous at the knee. Orange-red goes orange (2x), yellow (4x), pale yellow (8x), white (16x+); Cyans, blues and violets (OKLCh hues 190 to 300, fading out over 160 to 190 and 300 to 330) then take back their own hue at the curve's lightness and chroma, with the chroma lowered where the color would leave sRGB, and go back to the curve's luminance: Meta Blue stays a saturated blue until it whitens, and Cyan whitens toward a paler cyan, never green. Reds, oranges and yellows whiten through yellow the same way and stop at hue 100, 6 degrees past Yellow (sRGB's own path goes on to its yellow corner near 110): bright Yellow turns a paler golden yellow, not lemon. Pack colors that are already bright at nominal (Lime, Ice, and less so Cyan and Yellow) have little room above 1, so their glows still level off into a pale disc within a short distance. Where two channels tie for the largest, the rate that holds the luminance slope would shoot up and whiten the third channel in a thin ridge (Yellow over Cyan at 1.5x, blue along a row went 196, 208, 229, 221, 218; now 196, 203, 207, 215, 218). Within 5 % of a tie each lower channel's share of that rate blends toward a value both sides give alike, so the rate passes through the tie smoothly; colors farther from a tie come out exactly as before.

```sh
node scripts/light/audit.mjs --ref <id>[,<id>...] | --all
  [--preset <path>]   # with one --ref: audit this preset file instead
  [--size WxH ...]    # extra renders at these sizes
  [--export4k]        # also render at the 3840 px long edge with 1:1 crops
  [--isolines [6..10]] # draw Oklab lightness isolines over target and render (default 8)
  [--ss 1|2]          # supersample, default 2
  [--toe 0..1]        # set output.toe on every preset (to compare the paper toe)
  [--out <dir>]       # default artifacts/light-audit
```

For each reference, `artifacts/light-audit/<id>/`:

- `render.png`: the preset at the target's size.
- `compare.png`: target | render | Oklab ΔE heatmap (black 0, white 0.15). Below: row-mean color strips of target (top) and render (bottom), with x = row index, and row-mean Oklab L (solid) and chroma (dashed) for target (white) and render (orange).
- `metrics.json`: see below, plus timings.
- With `--isolines`: `isolines.png` (target | render with the same 6–10 Oklab lightness isolines) and the same pair as a last row of `compare.png`. Levels are evenly spaced between the target's 2nd and 98th lightness percentile; both images are blurred by 0.8 % of the short side first so grain and JPEG blocks do not draw their own loops. It shows whether the render's boundaries curve, drift and change spacing like the target's, or run straight and parallel.
- With `--size`: `render-WxH.png`. With `--export4k`: `export-4k.png`, `crop-smooth.png` (the gentlest ramp), `crop-bright.png`, `crop-dark.png`, each also as `*-contrast4x.png`.

`artifacts/light-audit/contact-sheet.png` shows target and render for every reference that has a `render.png` in the out dir.

```sh
node scripts/light/fit.mjs --ref <id>
  [--params a,b,c | all]      # numeric and vector params; `all` skips booleans and integer counts
  [--colors [all | slot,...]] # fit these color slots in Oklab
  [--lens bloom,halation,...] # numeric lens settings
  [--iters 400] [--restarts 2] [--size 256] [--weights image,rows,columns]
  [--preset <path>] [--write] [--out <dir>]
```

Nelder-Mead on values normalized to their ranges, with restarts from the best point at half the step. It renders at `--size` px on the long side (1x, no dither, no grain) in one browser session; the loss is computed in the page. Loss = image ΔE + 0.5 row-profile ΔE + 0.5 column-profile ΔE by default. Prints the loss before and after and writes `artifacts/light-fit/<id>/` (`compare.png`, `render.png` at full size, `preset.json`, `fit.json` with the loss history). Only `--write` overwrites the preset JSON. Look at `compare.png` before keeping a fit: a lower loss can still add a hard edge.

```sh
node scripts/light/profile.mjs [--recipe <id>[,<id>...]] [--pack bold] [--seed 3] [--size 1920x1080] [--ss 1]
  [--runs 5] [--defaults] [--json <file>] [--save <dir>] [--against <dir>]
```

Where a recipe's render time goes. Each recipe renders its compose scene (the pack, controls at 0.5, the frame's aspect; with `--defaults` or without a compose, the recipe defaults and the seed), then the same scene with a stub of its program whose shader functions return constants. It prints the time of single renders that wait for the GPU, the time per frame of 10 renders drawn back to back, and a table per stage (scatter sources, scatter blurs, radiance texture, post passes, lens base, lens bloom, lens halation, composite, resolve, output) with the total, the engine alone (the stub) and the difference, which is the recipe's own shader. Compare the back-to-back times: a GPU that sits idle between single renders slows down, so single renders vary more. Stage times come from renders that wait for the GPU after every stage, so they add up to more than a normal render. Other programs drawing on the GPU at the same time slow everything down, so compare runs made one after the other.

`--save <dir>` writes a 1080p 1x preview and a 4K 2x export of each scene (no grain, no dither) and prints preview vs export ΔE. `--against <dir>` renders again and prints ΔE against images saved earlier, which shows whether a speedup changed the image.

```sh
node scripts/light/look-switch.mjs [--start sunset|contour] [--no-parallel] [--url http://localhost:3000] [--label text] [--out dir]
```

Whether switching looks freezes the tool's page. Loads the tool from the dev server in a fresh Chrome profile (1280x800), with the drag hint hidden, and switches through all seven looks with the panel's keys, twice in one page and once in a second page of the same browser. The panel has no Look picker since 2026-11-09 (the seed picks the look, `src/tool/seedLook.ts`), so a step is Enter on Shuffle, with the page's next `Math.random` queued so that Shuffle lands on a chosen seed, or an arrow key on the Mood tiles; a line notes when the page shows another look than the step expects. Every fragment shader gets a line unique to the run, so the first page compiles from scratch and the second finds the browser's shader cache warm. For the page load and each switch it prints the longest main-thread task until the new look's settled frame (it should be 0), the time spent in compile status queries, GL calls over 5 ms by name, and the time to the first and the settled frame. It also prints the canvas's times for the new look's first 1x and 2x frames: `fastMs` and `settledMs` run from issuing a frame until it is on the canvas (whole animation frames), and `fastGpuMs` and `settledGpuMs` are the GPU timer's reading for the render. On this Mac that reading is 1 to 3 times the profile's stage times, so use it to compare looks, not as a cost. `--no-parallel` hides KHR_parallel_shader_compile, to see how a browser without it behaves (each new look's first frame then blocks the page while its shaders compile). A compile status query over 50 ms is saved with its stack. Writes `look-switch-<start>[-no-parallel].json` (default `artifacts/look-switch`). The page start (Bands, or Halo with `--start contour`) compiles in the page load, so run both starts to time every look's first switch.

```sh
node scripts/light/panel-pictures.mjs [--out public/panel] [--seed n] [--sheet file.jpg]
```

Renders the fixed pictures on the tool panel's Mood tiles into `public/panel/` (WebP): each mood's first look (the one seed 1 opens on) in the Atmospheric pack (`PACK_ID`) at 384x216 with the seed in `SEEDS`. ### Palette check

```sh
node scripts/light/palette-check.mjs --look <ramp|bands|blooms|rim|halo|beam|streaks|sunset.merged|contour.merged|rush.split|all>[,...]
  [--from <dir> | --dev [--url http://localhost:3000]] [--holdout] [--seeds 40] [--portrait 40] [--alike-seeds 8]
  [--maps] [--label text] [--out dir] [--drags owner,mirror,above,side|all|none] [--renders | --drag-renders | --all-renders]
  [--rule new|old] [--lens-edge 0.165] [--tails drift|strict] [--dim strict] [--haze strict|narrow] [--main bright|own]
  [--chroma 0.8,0.6] [--at intensity=1,softness=0,horizon=0.3] [--mix default|blue-off|Cyan:0.6 [--reading first]]
node scripts/light/palette-check.mjs --refs <id,...|sunset|contour|all> [--chroma 0.8,0.6] [--out dir]
```

Checks that every frame reads as its pack: the three packs at seeds 1-40 in both formats, at the default light and at four test drags, 1,200 frames a look (about 75 to 155 s a look from a frozen copy on this Mac; SHARED.md, 11:40 PM on 2026-11-06: agents check seeds 1-40 before each commit). `--holdout` checks seeds 41-80 instead, which nobody tunes against; `--seeds` and `--portrait` set how many seeds each format gets (`--seeds 8 --portrait 4` is the old, quick set). The summary lists the failing frames first, at every light and grouped by reason, then per pack the default light's results and per drag the counts. The sheets show the first 8 seeds at 16:9 and the first 4 at 9:16, then each row's failing frames from the other seeds (16 at most; the rest are in the summary). Seeds that look alike are counted among the first 8 seeds at 16:9 (`--alike-seeds`), as the looks' budgets are. Without `--from` or `--dev` it renders the tool's scene (controls at 0.5) with the harness from the checkout the script sits in; to check committed code, run it from a frozen copy of HEAD (`git archive HEAD src scripts public package.json tsconfig.json | tar -x -C <dir>`, with `node_modules` linked), which also gives it each frame's `scene.colors`. `--dev` shoots the dev server (one page at a time, the uncommitted working tree; say so and give the time in `--label`), with the drag hint ("Drag to reposition the gradient", shown in the middle of the image until a browser's first drag) hidden; the tool page sends its own fixed intensity, softness and mood control, so `--dev` takes no `--at`. `--from` reads `<look>-<pack>-s<seed>-<16x9|9x16>[-<drag>].png` from a folder. It writes `<out>/<recipe>.png` (packs as rows, seeds as columns, a bar under each tile with each pack color's share, clean mixes, pale bridges, white, the dark base and outside colors, failing tiles framed in red), `<out>/<recipe>.drags.jpg` (the same for each test drag), `summary.md` (the failing frames and why, and each look's time), `palette.json`, and with `--maps` a map per frame of what each pixel counted as. It keeps renders only when asked, since a full run's renders came to 70-220 MB (Engine, 2026-11-10): `--renders` keeps the default light's frames the sheets show (the first seeds and every failing frame) in `renders/`, `--drag-renders` those of the drags too, and `--all-renders` every frame (what `--from` and `moved-copies.mjs` read). Default `--out` is `artifacts/palette-check/<label>`.

The test drags (SHARED.md, "Every crop shows its pack", 2026-11-05 10:48 AM) are set as the tool sets them after a drag (`controlsForDrag`: the line, and the mood control from its start): the owner's line `from=1.011,1.224&to=-0.078,-0.067` (a Sunset frame all sky), its mirror `from=-0.011,1.224&to=1.078,-0.067`, a line high above pointing up `from=0.5,-0.3&to=0.5,-0.5` (all ground) and a sideways line `from=0.9,0.5&to=0.1,0.5`. The frame rules below apply at each drag, except that a Sunset frame under a drag follows the drag rule instead of the three-color rules (SHARED.md, 12:45 and 12:50 PM). With all four, a look takes 10 to 15 s (180 frames) from a frozen copy on this Mac, and all seven about 90 s; `--drags none` checks the default light only (36 frames, 2 to 4 s a look).

How a pixel counts:

- As a pack color when, at its Oklab lightness, it has the color's hue (within 20°) on the color's light path: the color under less light (the tone curve is the identity below nominal) up to its overexposed version through the engine's tone curve. Below nominal it may be at most 35% greyer than the path; pale tints (lighter and greyer, cream for Yellow) count down to L 0.78. Warm colors count down to 1 stop under nominal (Orange-red 1.5 stops), because dimmer they read as olive and brown (`docs/light/research/perception-additive-light.md` 1.4 and 4.5). Dark colors (L under 0.5) count up to 1 stop over nominal, because brighter they read as another color (Forest aquamarine, Plum hot pink). Atmospheric's Ice, Steel, Slate and Near-black share one hue and are told apart by lightness.
- As a cool light's haze (the default, from the 4:15 PM decision on 2026-11-04 in SHARED.md): below nominal, a Meta Blue or Cyan pixel greyer than the dim-color limit below still counts as that light while its Oklab chroma is 0.035 or more and its hue stays in the color's window (so the indigo limit still applies). That is the moodboard's haze: the beam pool's blue haze has a median chroma of 0.045 (10% under 0.030), and by eye a blue haze at 0.035 still reads blue-grey, at 0.03 grey (`artifacts/compare/rounds/12/haze/`). Where a pack grey fits better (Atmospheric's Steel and Slate), the grey wins. Warm lights keep the tail rule, and Plum, a dark base, is no light. In Elemental and Atmospheric, Meta Blue's haze, dim or paler than nominal, may turn up to 30° toward cyan (`T.hazeHue`; 2026-11-05), against 20° for the rest of its falloff; in Bold the hues between Meta Blue and Cyan are their clean mix, so the haze keeps the 20°: the beam pool's haze sits 15° to 28° from Meta Blue toward cyan (median 22°, halfway to Cyan), and by eye a haze at 30° still reads blue-grey next to it, at 35° teal (`artifacts/compare/refcheck/hue-swatches/sheet.jpg`). At the beam pool's median haze chroma (0.045) a turn of 30° moves the color by 0.023 in Oklab, about one just-noticeable step. `--haze narrow` keeps the haze in the dim window, as before; `--haze strict` turns the haze rule off.
- As a dim color (the default, from the 1:40 PM decision on 2026-11-04 in SHARED.md): a pack color that isn't warm and isn't near-grey (Meta Blue, Cyan, Plum) dimmed along its own falloff counts as that color all the way down to the dark base, so Meta Blue may sink to a deep navy. Deeper down it may be greyer: the share of the path's chroma it must keep falls from 65% at nominal to 40% at L 0.2 and below. A hue that moves away is outside: Meta Blue's falloff may turn at most 12° toward violet (20° toward cyan); from about 15° it reads indigo, and such a pixel is not a mix of Meta Blue with a color on the other side (Cyan, a grey) either. These pixels are named indigo (a third hue, so never a lens edge). The limits were set by eye on swatch sheets (`artifacts/compare/rounds/11/rule/swatches/`); the violet-wave reference's dark `#181751` sits at +18°. Warm colors keep the limits above and the tail rule below. `--dim strict` keeps the nominal limits all the way down, as before.
- As a clean mix only for the pairs that stay clean in linear light (the same doc, section 5): Meta Blue with Cyan, Forest, Plum or Ice, Yellow with Orange-red, and Atmospheric's greys. Every other pair goes grey, invents a hue (magenta, mint) or turns olive, khaki or brown.
- As a lens edge (the default rule, `--rule new`, from the palette rules in SHARED.md, 2026-11-03): an off-pack pixel on the linear-light mix of two pack colors that both show as areas near their own brightness, which is what the lens makes of an edge between them. It is fine where the band is no wider than the lens's own blur, 2R = 0.165 of the short side at Softness 0.5 (`--lens-edge`; R runs 0.045 to 0.12 with Softness); wider parts count as outside. Third hues (pink, salmon, mauve, mauve-grey, lavender, violet, magenta) are never lens edges, and in Contour the frame's dark base is never one side of one, so a light's tail over the base is still judged as a tail (C-025). `--rule old` turns lens edges off.
- As a warm light's tail (the default, `--tails drift`, from the 9:40 AM decision in SHARED.md): below nominal, an Orange-red, Yellow or Tan light that drifts toward deep red or amber as it dims, or a Lime light that drifts toward Forest's deep green, still counts as that light if it stays at least as pure (hues and chroma-to-lightness limits in `T.tails`). Olive, brown and grey tails are still outside. `--tails strict` turns this off. Yellow drifts like fire (10:40 AM decision), so its tail runs from amber through Orange-red's hue (about 37°) to deep red (about 29°). Where two tails reach a hue, the nearer color takes it, and in a Yellow-lit frame the Orange-red-colored part is Yellow's tail, not a drift into a second light (`T.tailPartners`; Lime's partner is Forest). A frame that reads as Orange-red-lit counts as Yellow-lit when Yellow covers a quarter or more of its lit area; Orange-red lights show no Yellow.
- As a pack color's own dim path inside its pack (coordinator, 2026-11-10 1:28 AM), for a pixel no rule above takes. These are the paths the engine's fire drift and Rush's dim-path look draw (`T.dimPaths`):
  - Bold: Yellow into Orange-red, then deep red (Oklab L 0.45, chroma 0.17, hue 29) and maroon (L 0.28, chroma 0.09, hue 18); Cyan into Meta Blue and navy.
  - Elemental: Lime into Forest, green (hue 150) by L 0.6; Forest and Plum toward black.
  - Atmospheric: Ice through Steel and Slate to Near-black.

  A path runs from anchor to anchor in Oklab lightness, chroma and hue, then to black. A pixel below the first anchor's lightness counts as the nearer named color when its a and b lie within 0.01 plus 0.15 times the path's chroma of the path at its own lightness (`T.dimPathWidth`). ### Moved copies

```sh
node scripts/light/palette-check.mjs --look <look> --all-renders --out <dir>
node scripts/light/moved-copies.mjs --from <dir> [--out <dir>/moved] [--across] [--structure 0.25] [--colors 0.15] [--layout 0.2]
```

Reports the pairs of a look's frames that are the same picture moved, scaled a little or mirrored (report only, SHARED.md, 2026-11-07: it fails nothing). The palette check's seed measure doesn't call them alike because it compares frames as they sit. It reads a palette-check output folder that has every frame's render (`--all-renders`; about 300 MB a look) and compares every pair of frames in the same look, pack, format and view (the default light or one test drag). `--across` also compares the looks of one mood in the folder (Sunset: Ramp, Bands, Blooms; Contour: Rim, Halo, Beam, Streaks).

A pair is judged by its structure: Oklab L at 80x45 (45x80 for 9:16), blurred by 1 px, with each frame's mean over the overlap taken off; the summed difference over the summed size of both. It is 0 when the light and dark places match, whatever the exposure, and 1 when they are unrelated. It is taken after the best move of the second frame onto the first: shifts up to 40% of the frame each way, sizes 1/1.15, 1 and 1.15, and mirrored left to right. In Contour it also tries top to bottom and both; a Sunset sky stays on top. Only the overlap counts. It must be 60% of the frame and hold 80% of each frame's structure (its L's distance from its own mean, so a dark ground counts as much as a light).

A pair is a moved copy when, after the move, its structure is 0.25 or less (`--structure`) and its seed colors are 0.15 or less (`--colors`), while unmoved its seed layout is over 0.2 (`--layout`), so the palette check doesn't already call it alike. Pairs that are as close without a move are `same unmoved`. Set by eye in compare round 18 (`artifacts/compare/rounds/18/moved/`): up to 0.25 the pairs are the same picture moved (Bands' blue band over haze shifted, Halo's arc moved or turned, Beam's shafts under a drag, the Contour top-edge glow high above); from 0.3 they are mostly not. Rim's thin lines are the weakest case: two lines 15 to 20 degrees apart can match at 0.22. The seed measure's own layout after a move was tried first and dropped: in pale frames it matched a blue band top-left with one on the right edge, and a move could drop a dark ground out of the overlap.

It writes into `<from>/moved/` (or `--out`):

- `moved.md`: per look, the moved copies at the run's first 8 seeds and at all its seeds, by pack, format and view;
- `moved.json`: every pair with its numbers and its move;
- `moved-<look>-s1-8.jpg` and `moved-<look>-s1-40.jpg` (`s41-48` and `s41-80` with `--holdout`): each pair as the first frame, the second, and the second moved onto the first. A sheet shows at most 48 pairs: all of them when there are fewer, otherwise an even sample per view.

One look (1,200 frames) takes about 80 s to check with `--all-renders` from a frozen copy on this Mac, then about 10 s for the moved copies. All seven looks with `--across` took 2.5 minutes in compare round 18, most of it reading the renders.

### Same design

```sh

  [--format 16x9,9x16,1x1,4x5] [--colors 1FCDF0,FFD61E,FF4F00] [--mix [default,blue-off,Cyan:0.6]] [--intensity 0.5]
```

Checks the owner's rule of 2026-11-09 (AGENTS.md): the same mood, seed, drag and format give the same design in every pack, and only the colors change. For each look, seed, format and view it renders the three packs at 320 px (2x supersampled) with the controls the tool sends, and compares every two frames on their lightness alone (Oklab L at 128x72), so the colors themselves don't count:

- layout: each pixel's lightness as its rank in its own frame (0 darkest, 1 brightest; steps under 0.06 count as equal), and how far that rank moves between the two frames, counted by how lit the pixel is in the brighter of them. A dark base both share counts little; a beam that moves counts fully.
- light: one minus the correlation of the two frames' lightness, each stretched from its darkest 2% to its brightest 0.5%. It is 0 when one is a brighter or dimmer copy of the other.

Each frame gets a mask of its meeting lines (`lib/design-maps.mjs`, `MEET`): pixels whose window, 0.03 of the short side to each side, holds two pack colors 90 degrees or more apart in hue (the engine's far hues, `src/light/engine/meet.ts`) as its two largest, the smaller at least 30% of the larger, widened by 0.06 of the short side (the engine's bridge out to two sigma). A dim room is dark, not a fill, and a band of a smooth gradient that reads as one color has soft borders. White (L 0.95 and up) is left out.
- **Dot.** One pack color's area of 0.05% to 2% of the frame, off the frame's edge, with 85% or more of a ring 1% to 2.5% of the short side out from it on one other color; the ring skips the thin blended edge round the dot. - **Seam.** A part of one label (a pack color, `grey` or `other`) 1.5% of the short side thick or thinner and 12% or longer. A light's hot line inside a later color of its own path, such as a Yellow core along an Orange-red streak, is the light's own. ## Metrics (`metrics.json`)

All ΔE values are plain Euclidean Oklab distances (L on 0..1; about 0.02 is one just-noticeable difference). The roadmap's starting targets are mean under 0.03 and p95 under 0.06. Ignore rects and rounded corners are masked everywhere.

- `deltaE`: mean, p95 and max per pixel after area-downsampling both images by 4 and blurring with a gaussian of 1 % of the short edge (removes grain, dither and JPEG blocks).
- `rowProfileDeltaE`, `columnProfileDeltaE`: ΔE between the mean colors of each row (column), averaged and max.
- `histogramDistance`: earth mover's distance between the Oklab L histograms and between the chroma histograms.
- `banding`: share of pixels on monotonic 8-bit steps at least 24 px long (0 none, 1 fully banded), target and render.
- `clipped`: share of pixels with a channel at 255 (`high`) or all channels at 0 (`low`).
- `grain`: robust standard deviation of luma minus its gaussian blur (sigma 2 px), in 8-bit code values.
- `falloff` (Contour references only; also printed under each audit line and on `compare.png`): how the glow falls off outward from the brightest light. The light is where the target, lightly blurred, is at least half its peak. At 0.01, 0.02, 0.04 and 0.07 of the short side from it, a thin ring is read in target and render on the same pixels (the ring's darker half by target luminance, i.e. the outer side of a rim or line), and the far ring at 0.15 is subtracted as the background. `ratio` is render glow / target glow per distance (above 1: the render's glow is too bright there); `target` and `render` are the glows as shares of each image's light level; `length` is the fitted exponential falloff length of each. A ratio that rises with distance means the render's glow reaches too far; one that falls means it ends too soon.

## Writing recipes and presets

Types are in `src/light/scene.ts`. A recipe file (one per recipe, no imports between recipe files) exports a `RecipeDefinition`: `id`, `mood`, `label`, `colorSlots`, `defaultColors`, `params` (`ParamSpec`: number, boolean or vector, each with key, label, range, step, default), `fragment`, `uniforms(ctx)`, optional `post` passes and `lens` / `output` defaults, and `fromPack(pack)`.

`fragment` is GLSL ES 3.00 that defines `vec3 radiance(vec2 uv, vec2 p)` and returns scene-linear light (may exceed 1). `uv` is the frame position (0..1, y down); `p` is the offset from the frame center in short-side units (y down). Write sizes in short-side units so a preset looks the same at every resolution. The engine prepends its uniforms (`uRenderSize`, `uFrameAspect`, `uPixel`, `uSeed`, ...) and the chunk library in `src/light/engine/glsl/` (hashes, noise, fbm, domain warp, dither, falloffs, SDFs, color helpers, `blurredRamp`, `blurredBands`, `sampleSmooth`). `uniforms(ctx)` maps resolved params and linear slot colors to uniform values; arrays may be passed flat. `ctx.aspect` is the frame's width / height, for CPU work that depends on the frame (such as calibrating boundaries); `uniforms`, `radianceScale`, post-pass settings and scatter radii all get it. A frame within 2 % of an export format's aspect gets the format's exact value (`frameAspect` in `src/light/engine/frame.ts`, the same number compose gets), so a preview, which the tool sizes in whole pixels, and the export of that format see the same aspect and draw the same picture.

Scattered light (`scatter`, for light that a medium inside a shape catches). List up to 4 blurs, each `{ source: 'radiance' | 'aux', radius: (ctx) => sigma }` with the gaussian sigma in short-side units. The fragment then also defines

```glsl
vec3 combine(vec2 uv, vec2 p, vec3 radiance, vec3 blurred0 /*, vec3 blurred1, ... one per entry, in order */)
```

and what it returns is the radiance that post passes, the lens and the tone curve see. `combine` runs at full resolution wherever radiance is evaluated, so it can mask the scattered light with the recipe's own shapes (for example `radiance + k * tint * insideMask(p) * blurred0`). Source `'aux'` blurs `vec3 auxRadiance(vec2 uv, vec2 p)` instead of `radiance`: a second output for blurring only part of the light. It can hold any non-negative data, one value per channel; for example each ring's light in its own channel, so each disc can catch only the other rings' light (`test.scatter` in `scripts/light/harness/testPrograms.ts` does this for two rims). The sources render at a working size whose short side keeps the smallest blur at least 4 px (256 to 1024 px, independent of the output size, so previews and exports scatter the same light); `radiance` sources are clamped at 0, aux values are blurred as they are. Cost: one source render per source kind, one blur per entry, and one cubic texture read per entry per pixel.

An entry can also set `size: (ctx) => px`, the short side of its own source (8 to 2048), instead of the shared working size. Like that size it does not depend on the output size. A sized source takes one sample per texel, so it is for layers that are smooth at that size, and with `radius: () => 0` it is not blurred: `{ source: 'aux', radius: () => 0, size: () => 192 }` renders `auxRadiance` once at 192 px on the short side and `combine` reads it back with a cubic filter. That is a cache for an expensive smooth layer such as a sky. In `check.mjs` a deliberately slow layer read from a 128 px cache matches the same layer at every pixel within mean ΔE 0.0001 and takes 0.9 instead of 11.9 ms at 1920x1080 1x.

`shape: 'lens'` makes an entry one out-of-focus lens over its source instead of a gaussian: `radius` is the disc radius R in short-side units (Sunset's Softness runs 0.045 to 0.12, 0.0825 at the default), and the kernel matches `prototypes/organic-principle/lib/lens.mjs` with rim 0.35 and stretch 1.1: 65 % a disc of radius R with a one-texel rim plus 35 % a gaussian of sigma R / 2, both 10 % wider than tall, normalized over the taps, in linear light. The source renders at 4 x 4 pixels per grid texel, one sample each, and is averaged down to a grid whose short side is ceil(6 / R), so R is about 6 texels and sharp outlines are averaged over 16 samples before the gather. Past the frame edges it repeats the grid's edge texels, like the other entries. `combine` reads it back with the cubic filter; the grid follows R and the frame's aspect only, so a preview and a 4K export read the same light. For a whole-frame defocus, scatter `radiance` with the lens and return only the blurred light from `combine`:

```ts
scatter: [{ source: 'radiance', radius: (ctx) => ctx.num('lensRadius'), shape: 'lens' }],
// fragment: vec3 combine(vec2 uv, vec2 p, vec3 light, vec3 blurred0) { return blurred0; }
```

The shader compiler then drops the unused full-resolution `radiance` from the composite, so the scene is evaluated only on the grid (16 x 9,500 times at the default R for 16:9). In `check.mjs` a slow scene costs 2.7 ms at 1080p that way and 23 ms when `combine` also reads `light`. A horizontal edge comes out 1.37 to 1.39 R wide (10 to 90 %; the CPU kernel alone gives 1.35 R, the cubic read adds the rest), a band 3 R tall keeps its exact hex in the middle, and the result matches the CPU prototype within a mean of 0.07 to 0.21 Oklab ΔE×100. A vertical edge is 10 % wider (the stretch).

A preset is `{ id, referenceId, scene: { recipe, seed, colors, params, lens, output } }`. Missing params take the recipe defaults, missing colors the recipe's default colors.

Lower-resolution radiance (`radianceScale: 0.5` or `0.25`, or a function of the scene that returns one): for smooth looks. The recipe's radiance renders once at that share of the render resolution, one sample per pixel, and the composite upsamples it with a cubic B-spline before the lens, tone curve and grain; the lens base is made from the same texture instead of evaluating the recipe again. At 1920x1080 1x it takes sunset.ramp from about 21 to 4.5 ms, sunset.blooms from 15 to 5.6 ms and contour.beam from 26 to 4.8 ms, with p95 ΔE 0.0001 against full resolution and the same preview-to-export match (`check.mjs`, `speed`). Features narrower than about 2 / scale render pixels get soft, so keep sharp cores, riso ink edges and thin lines at 1.

Per-pixel grain weight (`grainWeight: true`, and the fragment defines `float grainWeight(vec2 uv, vec2 p)` returning 0..1): it multiplies the grain at each point, so a riso recipe can put strong grain in dense dark ink and less in thin ink and on paper. It travels in the alpha channel to the output pass, also through post passes.

`output.toe` is the paper toe from `docs/light/research/light-photography.md`, 0 (off, the default) to 1: below nominal the weaker channels fall faster than the largest, so dim Yellow reads amber instead of olive, dim Lime a purer green, dim Orange-red deep red and dim blue navy. Light at and above nominal is unchanged. With the toe on, the engine pre-inverts every slot color (`ctx.colors`, `ctx.color`), so flat pack colors still print their exact hex (`check.mjs`, 12 of 12); a color a recipe builds from `ctx.hex` itself is not pre-inverted. Checks that every slider moves the image smoothly. Each jump gets a strip in `jumps/` (a JPEG: the frames two steps either side, the ΔE per step, and a ΔE map of the jump, white at its 99th percentile) and a line naming the scene params and uniforms that jump with it (those whose change across the jump is more than 4 times their usual change per step, or that switch where they otherwise hold still), which points at the code to fix; "nothing in the scene or uniforms" means the shader itself turns a smooth input into a fast change. `curves/<look>-<format>.png` plots the change per step against the control for every control and pack, one line per seed, log scale, jumps in red. `steps.json` holds every step and jump. `--frames 0:1:0.1` also writes a strip per sweep of frames at those values (`frames/`) and a contact sheet per look, control and format with a row per pack and seed (`contact/`), `--strip-all` a strip around every sweep's largest step. The run exits 1 when it finds a jump or a look does not load.

Line sweeps (`--control line`, or any of `startx,starty,angle,length`) check the dragged line (`ControlValues.drag`, SHARED.md "Dragging the light") the same way, with the same jump rule. Each starts from a base line (`--line x,y,angle,length`, repeatable: the start in frame units, the angle in degrees with 0 pointing right and -90 up, the length in short sides, so a line keeps its length on screen whichever way it points). `startx` and `starty` move the whole line so its start steps by 0.01 of the frame from -0.25 to 1.25 (0 to 1 with `--quick`); `angle` turns it about its start by 0.01 of a full turn; `length` grows it by 0.01 of the short side from 0.05 to 1.25 (0.1 to 1 with `--quick`). The scene is what the tool shows for that drag: `controlsForDrag` in `src/tool/controls.ts` (the line, clamped as the tool clamps it, and the mood control set from its start). The default run uses three base lines (start 0.35,0.7 at -40 degrees, length 0.6; 0.7,0.35 at 150, 0.4; 0.5,0.92 at -90, 0.9) and `--quick` the first. Strips and sheets of line sweeps draw the line on each frame (white with a dark edge, a ring at the start). A look that reads `drag` runs `--quick --look <look> --control line` (7,092 frames, about 80 s) before committing.

`--mix default` (or `blue-off`, or `Cyan:0.6`, as in the color checks) draws every frame as the tool does with `colormix=1`. The design is drawn in the color stage's design pack (`COLOR_DESIGN_PACK`), and the line's controls come from that pack too. The stage then gives it the frame's pack colors at that mix. A color that flips as the line moves is then a jump like any other. Each jump also gives the share of its worst block's change that is color rather than lightness (Σ|Δab| / (Σ|ΔL| + Σ|Δab|)), so a jump over 50% is mostly a color flip. The scene and uniforms don't show the stage's choices, so a color flip says "nothing in the scene or uniforms". The tool check sends `colormix=1` and the mix too. Without `--mix` the default out folder is the same as before; with it, the folder name ends in `-mix`. The looks behind a switch in the tool (`sunset.merged`, `contour.merged`, `rush.split`) are swept when named in full, with files named `sunset-merged-...` and so on. `--worst N` writes strips only for the N largest jumps (by mean ΔE) of each look and format, after the sweeps, for runs that find many.

`--control slider` (`afterdrag` and `moodalone`) checks the mood slider with a line set, from each base line, mood 0 to 1 by 0.01. `afterdrag` moves the line with the slider as the tool does: from the value the drag gave the slider, one 0.01 step at a time, both ends move by as much as the look's light moves along its slider path for that step, rounded once to the 0.001 steps the URL keeps. Every step should be a normal small step, with no jump and no stretch where nothing moves. `moodalone` holds the line still while the mood control changes: a look that takes its light's place, direction and reach from the line alone doesn't change at all, so every step should be 0; a look that also moves something by the mood control while a line is set moves the light twice when the user moves the slider after a drag.

```sh
node scripts/light/control-sweep.mjs --grid [--look ramp,...] [--pack bold] [--seeds 1-3] [--format 16x9] [--out dir]
```

`--reach R` runs the `startx` and `starty` sweeps from -R to 1 + R, so the line's start moves past the image's edges (a drag may start anywhere in the stage around the image; `POINT_REACH` in `src/tool/controls.ts` is how far past the edges the tool keeps it). `--grid --off 0.25,0.5` writes, instead of the two sheets below, one sheet per look and distance, `<look>-off<d>-<format>.jpg`: per pack and seed a 3 x 3 block with the seed's own frame in the middle and, around it, the line starting that far off each edge and corner of the image, where it sits, running to the image's middle (`--off-to mirror`: across the image to the point opposite its start, `-across` in the file name). Captions give each frame's mean Oklab L, so a frame gone dark stands out.

Two sheets per look for the dragged line, to look at (default out `artifacts/control-sweep/grid`): `<look>-directions-<format>.jpg`, per pack and seed a 3 x 3 block with the seed's own frame in the middle and the line from 0.5,0.55 with length 0.5 short sides in 8 directions, each frame where its direction points; and `<look>-lengths-<format>.jpg`, per pack and seed the seed's own frame and the line from 0.5,0.88 pointing up with length 0.25, 0.5 and 0.9. Captions give the mood control the tool sets from the line's start.

```sh
node scripts/light/drag-check.mjs [--url http://localhost:3000] [--look ramp,rim,...] [--pack bold] [--seed 3] [--format 16x9]
  [--checks] [--steps 120] [--step-ms 8] [--out dir]
```

Drags a line on the tool page in system Chrome (1440x900 at 2x, so the preview renders 2152x1210 at 1x while dragging) with real pointer input: per look one drag whose end circles its start nearer and farther, with a mouse move every 8 ms sent without waiting, like a trackpad. It prints the time between animation frames (median, p95, frames over 25 ms), the preview's draws per second, the canvas's `fastMs`, and shader compiles during the drag (there should be none; a dev-server module update from another agent's edit landing mid-drag is named as the cause when it happens), and checks that the URL has the dragged line. `--checks` also tests in Ramp and Rim: the grab cursor and the closed hand, a click doing nothing, the line showing while dragging and going on release, the URL changing only when the drag ends, Shuffle keeping a Sunrise line (a Contour line stays or goes, as the new seed's look can reach its start, and never moves), double-click clearing it, touch and pen drags (no scroll, no text selection), and no page errors. The panel has no mood sliders since 2026-11-09, so their checks are gone; looks open by their URL. It never starts a dev server. Writes `report.json` (default `artifacts/drag-check`); exits 1 on a failed check or a compile during a drag.

`--quick` runs seeds 1-6 at 16:9 for all three controls and packs (5,454 frames per look, about a minute) and is the check to run before committing a change to a look's controls or compose. The full default run (7 looks, seeds 1-12 and 5081, 16:9 and 9:16) takes about 13 minutes for the Sunset looks and 16 for Contour. Other agents edit the working tree while a sweep runs, so `--rev <commit>` renders from a copy of that commit (`git archive` of `src scripts package.json tsconfig.json` in the system temp folder, `node_modules` linked; each run makes its own and deletes it at the end) and `--patch <file>` applies a patch to that copy first, to test a change against HEAD alone; `--tree` renders from a copy of the working tree as it is at the start. A frame takes 6 to 12 ms to render (320x180 at 2x), more for the first frames of a look while its shader compiles.

`output.grain` is the grain's Density 0..1 (`src/light/engine/grain.ts`): fine specks on Oklab L at output size, after the tone curve and before dither, like Figma's Noise effect. A speck is one pixel at 1080 px on the short side (about 2 px with soft edges in a 4K export); each output pixel averages the specks over its area, so a smaller preview shows what the export shows at that size. Density is the share of cells with a speck: 0 is off, 1 a speck in every cell. Each speck is lighter or darker by the same amount at every Density, so the mean stays: Density 0.5 is about 1.7 % Oklab L on an L 0.45 grey in a 4K export, Density 1 about 2.4 %. It fades to 0 at black and near white. Older presets' `grainSize` (px) and `grainChroma` are ignored.

### Output filters: grain, then Pixel, then dither

The output pass (`src/light/engine/output.ts`) runs a chain of filter stages on every output pixel, after the Mitchell resolve and the tone curve and before the sRGB encode and the dither. Each stage has a strength in the output settings, 0..1: `output.grain`, `output.pixel`. A stage at 0 is left out of the shader. The ASCII filter was removed on 2026-11-09 (owner). Stages run in the order of `OUTPUT_FILTERS` in `src/light/engine/filters/index.ts` (grain first). The tool turns on one filter at a time; the engine runs whatever is above 0.

To plug in a filter:

1. Write `src/light/engine/filters/<name>.ts` exporting an `OutputFilter` (`filters/types.ts`):
   - `key`: the stage's name, added to `FilterKey` in `filters/types.ts` and to `OutputSettings` in `src/light/scene.ts`.
   - `glsl`: the stage's uniforms and helpers (prefix them with the stage's name) and `vec3 <apply>(vec3 display, ivec2 px, float weight)`. `display` is display-linear color, 0..1 per channel, in and out. `px` is the output pixel, x right and y down. `weight` is the recipe's grain weight (most stages ignore it). The pass has the chunk library and `uniform int uSeed;`. Size everything in fractions of the short side, so a preview and an export show the same cells.
   - `apply`: the function's name.
   - `uniforms(strength, { width, height, seed })`: the stage's uniforms at that output size, for a strength above 0.
   - `source` (optional), for stages that need per-cell values: `{ uniform, size(frame, strength), samples? }`. The engine renders the whole frame once at `size` texels (each the mean of `samples` x `samples` samples, 1 to 8, 4 by default; `samples` may also be a function of `(frame, strength)`; display-linear after the tone curve, before any filter; alpha is the grain weight; row 0 is the top) and binds it to the sampler `uniform`. Make `size` depend on the frame's aspect and the strength only, so previews and exports read the same texels.
2. Add it to `OUTPUT_FILTERS` in `filters/index.ts`, after `GRAIN_FILTER`. That file belongs to the grain agent; adding your entry and its import is fine.
3. Render with `output: { <key>: 0.5 }` through the harness and look at the image.

`OUTPUT_FILTER_SHADERS` lists the output pass for no filter and for each filter alone, for compiling them ahead. `prepareOutputFilters(gpu, output, frame, drawFrame)` builds the pass for a scene: its shader, the active stages' uniforms and their frame sources. Until `programRenderer.ts` calls it (a patch is with the coordinator), the renderer draws `OUTPUT_SHADER`, which holds the grain stage only, so only grain runs.

### Grain measurement

```sh
node scripts/light/grain-measure.mjs --refs                 # the moodboard images with grain, about 15 s
node scripts/light/grain-measure.mjs --renders [--only ramp,preview,riso,scenes,checks] [--looks ramp,beam] [--seed 1]
node scripts/light/grain-measure.mjs --sheets                # after --renders
node scripts/light/grain-measure.mjs --file <png> [--rect x,y,w,h] [--hp px]
```

One measurement for reference images and renders (`lib/grain.mjs`). The grain is each plane minus its gaussian blur at 0.65 % of the short side (6 px at 925 px, 14 px at 4K), on the pixels away from edges, folds and text. It reports the standard deviation of that residual in Oklab L (% of L) and in 8-bit luma (code values), overall and for shadows (L under 0.35), mids and highlights (over 0.7) and per 0.1 of L; the clump size as the half width of the residual's autocorrelation in px and per 1000 of the short side (white per-pixel noise about 0.5 px); the share of residual power above 0.25 cycles per px (about 0.8 for per-pixel noise, near 0 for soft clumps); the mono share var(L) / (var(L) + var(a) + var(b)); skew and dark specks (residuals 3 standard deviations below), which tell dark speckle from film grain; and banding as the share of pixels in runs of 16 px or more of one 8-bit color across which the blurred luma changes by more than half a code value.

`--refs` measures the three riso prints, the poster, a sky, the rings photo, the riso scan behind the prints (from `prototypes/grain-study/out/raw/`, when there) and the deck's Grain tile (44:271) at 1x, 2x and 4x with its source photo (downloaded to `artifacts/grain/figma/`). `--renders` measures, through the engine harness on the working tree: a black-to-white ramp at 1080p and 4K, preview against export, the riso reference presets at their print's size, every look and pack at 4K (Sunset Ramp and Contour Beam also at 1080p and grain 0 and 1), seed repeatability, repeats across the frame and banding on a dark ramp. `--sheets` puts 1:1 crops of those renders next to crops of the riso prints and the deck tile, each with its numbers. Everything goes to `artifacts/grain/measure/` (`refs.md`, `renders.md`, `crops/`, `sheets/`). Results: `docs/light/research/grain.md`.

`compose` (optional on a `RecipeDefinition`): `({ pack, controls: { intensity, softness, mood }, seed, aspect }) => { colors, params, lens, output }`, with each control 0..1 and `aspect` = width / height. The tool uses it instead of its fallback mapping in `src/tool/controls.ts`. Colors must be exact hexes of `pack` (or `#000000`); `compositionProblems(recipe, pack, composition)` in `src/light/compose.ts` lists anything wrong, and `src/light/recipes/compose.test.ts` runs it for every recipe with `compose` over 3 packs x 5 seeds x 4 aspects x 27 control settings.
